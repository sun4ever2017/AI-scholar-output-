# Own reading notes (lead reviewer), written before seeing the six agent reviews

## Biggest concerns
1. RQ–design mismatch. (B) asks "how much does generative AI raise output"; (K) concedes field×quarter FE absorb the common component, so only between-method differences are identified. Table 2 and the "AI part of that group's output growth" sentence read as a decomposition of total growth, which the design cannot deliver. Either re-centre the RQ on "which methods, when, for whom" or add a design for the total (low-exposure comparison group on SSRN, e.g., humanities/law networks; or Arcila-style cross-field).
2. Pre-period contamination. Sample selected on ≥2 papers in 2018–2021; pre-trend window is 2018–2022. Selection on pre-period output makes 2018–2021 pre-trends partly mechanical; the clean pre-period is 2022Q1–Q3 (three quarters). Fix: select on earlier activity (e.g., first SSRN paper ≤2017 or ≥2 papers in 2015–2018) and measure Share on 2015–2019; hold out 2020–2022Q3 as the pre-test window. Also COVID 2020–21 differentially hit experiments/field work.
3. Release-type attribution is fragile. Three nested step functions within ~2 years; the calendar is contestable (Copilot 2021–22, GPT-4 Mar 2023, Claude 3.5 Sonnet Jun 2024, o1 Sep/Dec 2024, DeepSeek-R1 Jan 2025, Claude Code Feb 2025). Present as a pre-registered event study by method with named windows; treat release labels as interpretation; drop "without assuming it in advance" (hypotheses do assume it).
4. Inflation test lacks power as designed. 24-month publication in ABDC A*/A or FT50 when accounting submit-to-accept ≈26 months (Hurley et al. 2025) and finance similar → base rate near zero, cannot fall. Use broader outcomes: any journal; conference acceptance; "forthcoming" status; citations; and longer windows for older cohorts.
5. Wald ratio is not an IV. Exclusion restriction (release affects output only through AI vocabulary) fails. Call it a descriptive scaling or drop.
6. SSRN posting-rate control cannot be measured for recent years (publication lags); for post-2023 cohorts the denominator (journal articles) is not yet observed. Reframe as a check on 2018–2022 only, plus the any-platform outcome.
7. Author-level panel on OpenAlex author IDs is risky (splitting/merging); need ORCID and name-institution matching, and report sensitivity.
8. H1 inconsistency: (C) says archival is easiest to automate "a prediction this project tests (H1)", but H1 says writing release → review/commentary; coding release → archival. Make H1 one clear statement.
9. For coauthors: the pitch never states the ask (what skills/data/time needed, authorship, division of labour). Add a short "What I am looking for" section.
10. Length: 4,400 words is 2–3× a Faff pitch. Move Table 2, the SSRN-growth paragraph detail and the congestion bullets to an appendix; keep the body ≤2,200 words.

## Things to keep
- Fixed, predetermined method mix as exposure (avoids the Renault critique) is the right core idea.
- Field-specific quarter FE, fixed panel, PPML, event-study pre-trends.
- The honest limitations paragraph.
- Data section is unusually concrete (OpenAlex/Crossref routes, licensing, ethics).

## Suggestions to elevate significance
- Make heterogeneity by evaluation regime (ABDC/ABS list schools vs US) a headline, not H4; the author's Australian vantage point is a comparative advantage.
- Classify post-2022 papers by method too, so the paper can show where the extra papers land (paper-level composition), complementing the scholar-level ITT.
- Title alternatives: "Which Research Does Generative AI Speed Up? Evidence from Finance and Accounting Working Papers"; "More Papers, Which Papers? Generative AI and Finance and Accounting Research".
